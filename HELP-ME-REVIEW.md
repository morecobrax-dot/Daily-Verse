# Help Me — review packet

Two pilot paths, eight steps, written in Phase A to establish the voice before
the remaining five paths are authored. **Nothing in this is user-visible.**
There is no Help Me tab, no reader and no stored progress; the content exists
so it can be reviewed before any of that is built.

Source: [data/help.json](data/help.json). Checks: `npm run help:verify`, and
CONTRACT 49 in the suite.

## What the automated checks already prove

So that review time goes where a machine cannot help:

- Every reference resolves, with real text, in **all seven shipped editions**.
- **No Scripture text is stored** in the file, and no prose reproduces six
  consecutive words of any anchor or context passage **in any of the three
  shipped English editions**.
- No prose claims private revelation, arranged circumstances, a promised
  outcome, a guaranteed feeling, or that distress proves weak faith.
- No prose diagnoses anybody, names a condition a reader "has", or counsels
  staying within reach of harm.
- No number to dial appears outside the verified crisis block.
- No two readings, arrivals or prayers open the same way.
- Nothing is near its length ceiling (largest step: 388 words of 520).

**None of that says the writing is true, wise or pastorally right.** That is
what this packet is for.

## What needs a person, ranked

1. **cb-5** — how the warning in Hebrews 10:26–31 is handled. Assurance and
   perseverance are a real denominational divide, and the reader of this path
   is unusually likely to read on and apply it to themselves.
2. **cb-4** — whether the groaning in Romans 8:26 is the Spirit's or the
   believer's. The reading takes a side in one clause.
3. **cb-3** — confession is described without saying where or to whom. Confirm
   that satisfies both a Protestant and a sacramental reader.
4. **cb-1** — a national covenant text applied to one person. The reading
   says so explicitly; confirm the caveat is enough.
5. **sh-2, cb-2** — two first-century cultural claims (the rabbi's yoke; an
   older man running). Both are standard, neither is in the text.

## Start Here — "I don't know where to start"

Three steps. Ends by handing the reader to **Learn → Learning to Pray**.

| Step | Passage | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| sh-1 Begin where you actually are | Psalm 62:5–8 | Psalm 62:1–4, 62:9–12 |
| sh-2 An invitation, not a summons | Matthew 11:28–30 | Matthew 11:20–24, 11:25–27 |
| sh-3 Something ordinary to do next | Luke 11:1–4 | Luke 11:5–10, 11:11–13 |

### sh-1 — Begin where you actually are

- **Thesis.** A psalm written under pressure tells its own soul to wait, then
  tells everyone else to bring God what they actually have.
- **Interpretive move.** Reads v8 as instruction rather than consolation, and
  takes "pour out" in its ordinary sense of emptying a vessel.
- **Application.** You may start without the right words or a diagnosis of
  your problem.
- **Prayer purpose.** Models starting badly on purpose.
- **Next step.** Continue to sh-2 — the path has not yet said who is being
  addressed.
- **Safety.** None.
- **Denominational.** None.
- **Look at:** *"The verb is the one you would use for tipping a jar until
  nothing is left in it."* A lexical claim stated plainly; confirm you are
  happy to assert it without a footnote. Also *"people are lighter than a
  breath"* — a description of v9, close to its wording by design, and checked
  against the six-word guard.

### sh-2 — An invitation, not a summons

- **Thesis.** The welcome is issued by someone who has just spoken severely,
  and what is offered is a shared yoke rather than the absence of a load.
- **Interpretive move.** Reads the yoke as a discipleship idiom, and the
  ground of the invitation as Christ's character rather than the reader's
  readiness.
- **Application.** Come to Christ before anything is sorted out.
- **Prayer purpose.** Coming as you are, in one sentence, without a request
  attached.
- **Next step.** Continue to sh-3.
- **Safety.** The word "rest" is the obvious place a reader could hear a
  promise about circumstances. The last paragraph refuses that explicitly.
- **Denominational.** None.
- **Look at:** *"Teachers of his day spoke of taking a rabbi's yoke, meaning
  you became his apprentice"* — standard background, not in the text.
  *"This is the one place in the Gospels where he describes his own heart"* —
  checked against the shipped text: Matthew 11:29 is the only verse in the
  four Gospels where Jesus speaks of his own heart. Keep or soften.

### sh-3 — Something ordinary to do next

- **Thesis.** Not knowing how to pray was ordinary among the people closest to
  Jesus, and his answer was short, plain words.
- **Interpretive move.** Reads "bread for today" as teaching return rather
  than sufficiency, and the placement of forgiveness mid-prayer as evidence it
  is meant to be routine.
- **Application.** Pray something short today, and again tomorrow.
- **Prayer purpose.** A prompt, not a written prayer: the passage is already a
  prayer, and competing with it would be odd.
- **Next step.** Today's reading — the path deliberately ends by sending the
  reader into the ordinary rhythm rather than deeper into Help Me.
- **Safety.** None.
- **Denominational.** Whether the Lord's Prayer is recited or used as a
  pattern differs by tradition; the step treats it as a pattern and does not
  prescribe. Note also that a reader in Reina-Valera 1909 sees a longer form
  than a WEB reader, because the editions differ here. The prose describes the
  petitions rather than quoting them, so it is true of both.
- **Look at:** *"read aloud, it takes about fifteen seconds"* — a small
  factual claim about the shorter form.

## Coming Back to God — "I feel far from God"

Five steps. Ends by handing the reader to **Today**.

| Step | Passage | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| cb-1 Come back with words | Hosea 14:1–4 | Hosea 14:1–9, 11:1–4 |
| cb-2 Met before you arrive | Luke 15:17–24 | Luke 15:1–3, 15:25–32 |
| cb-3 Say it, and let it be said | 1 John 1:8–10 | 1 John 1:5–10, 2:1–2 |
| cb-4 When you cannot pray | Romans 8:26–27 | Romans 8:18–25, 8:28–30 |
| cb-5 Keep going, and not alone | Hebrews 10:19–25 | Hebrews 10:11–18, 10:26–31 |

### cb-1 — Come back with words

- **Thesis.** A book of accusation ends with directions home, and God supplies
  the words to people who have none.
- **Interpretive move.** Takes "take words with you" as the centre, and reads
  the renunciation in v3 as what makes returning specific rather than vague.
- **Application.** Name what you have been relying on instead.
- **Prayer purpose.** Returning without a good explanation.
- **Next step.** Continue to cb-2 — the path has stated the turn but not yet
  what meets it.
- **Safety.** The opening refuses the idea that a reader must arrive repaired.
- **Denominational.** None directly.
- **Look at:** the paragraph beginning *"What this passage is doing, it should
  be said, is addressing a nation under a covenant"*. That caveat is the whole
  defence against reading a national promise as a personal guarantee. Confirm
  it is placed well and worded strongly enough. Also *"the answer is medical
  and free"* — healing imagery drawn from v4.

### cb-2 — Met before you arrive

- **Thesis.** The father moves first, at a distance, and restores the son as a
  son rather than accepting his offer to work.
- **Interpretive move.** Reads the rehearsed speech as an attempt to earn
  re-entry, and the father's interruption of it as the point of the parable.
  Keeps the elder brother's unresolved complaint in view rather than ending on
  the party.
- **Application.** Receiving is harder than earning, and that is the thing
  being asked.
- **Prayer purpose.** Says the true half of the speech and drops the bargain.
- **Next step.** Continue to cb-3.
- **Safety.** *"Coming back is not made cost-free for everyone around it"* is
  deliberate: a reader with real damage behind them should not be told the
  story ends tidily.
- **Denominational.** The parable is used in justification debates; the
  reading stays with the narrative and does not enter them.
- **Look at:** *"an older man in a long robe runs, which was not what dignity
  looked like in that world"* — cultural background, widely held, not in the
  text. *"he had been watching the road"* — an inference from "while he was
  still far off", stated as fact.

### cb-3 — Say it, and let it be said

- **Thesis.** Confession is agreement, and forgiveness is described as just
  because its ground is named in the next paragraph.
- **Interpretive move.** Takes *homologeō* as "say the same thing", and reads
  "faithful and righteous" against 2:1–2 rather than as mere leniency.
- **Application.** Name one thing plainly, today, without the rehearsed
  explanation.
- **Prayer purpose.** A prompt rather than a written prayer, because the
  reader should say their own words here.
- **Next step.** Continue to cb-4.
- **Safety.** Explicitly makes no promise about how confession will feel.
  Shame is never the mechanism.
- **Denominational.** **Flagged.** The passage does not say where or to whom
  confession is made; the step says so and declines to adjudicate. A
  sacramental reader and a free-church reader should both be able to use it.
  *"the just thing for God to do rather than the generous thing"* leans on
  propitiation; confirm the phrasing.
- **Look at:** *"righteous — just, in some translations"*. Added so the step
  does not explain an English word as though it were the text (CLAUDE.md rule
  49), since the shipped editions differ here.

### cb-4 — When you cannot pray

- **Thesis.** Inability to pray is the ordinary condition of people living
  before everything is put right, and the Spirit carries what cannot be said.
- **Interpretive move.** Reads "weakness" from the surrounding paragraph about
  unfinished things rather than as a character fault, and **attributes the
  wordless groaning to the Spirit**.
- **Application.** Bring back the thing you stopped bringing because you had
  no words for it.
- **Prayer purpose.** Prays the admission itself.
- **Next step.** Open Romans 8 and read from v18, so two verses are not read
  as a slogan.
- **Safety.** The closing sentence refuses the inference that silence in
  prayer means God has gone — the specific conclusion this reader is most
  likely to draw.
- **Denominational.** v29–30 (predestination) sit in the basis and are used
  only to define what "good" means in v28. Confirm that restraint is right.
- **Look at:** *"the Spirit's own intercession is made in groans too deep for
  words"*. Commentators divide on whose groaning this is. The step states one
  reading without signalling the other. Decide whether to keep it, qualify it,
  or move the weight onto the part nobody disputes.

### cb-5 — Keep going, and not alone

- **Thesis.** Confidence to draw near rests on a finished sacrifice, and the
  instructions that follow are plural: hold on, and do not abandon the
  gathering.
- **Interpretive move.** Reads "do not give up meeting together" as addressed
  to those present about how to treat the absent, not as a rebuke to the
  absent. Then names the warning that follows and refuses to apply it to a
  returning reader.
- **Application.** Tell one Christian you are coming back.
- **Prayer purpose.** Asks for people, not feelings.
- **Next step.** Human support — deliberately the one step that sends the
  reader toward a person rather than a screen.
- **Safety.** The warning paragraph is the single most likely thing in either
  pilot to harm someone. The step raises it first, rather than leaving the
  reader to meet it alone.
- **Denominational.** **Flagged, highest.** Hebrews 10:26–31 divides
  traditions on perseverance, apostasy and mortal sin. The step says Christians
  read it in genuinely different ways and sends the reader to a pastor.
- **Look at:** the whole final paragraph, word by word — *"It describes a
  deliberate walking away from the sacrifice — the opposite direction from the
  one you are walking in by reading this at all."* That sentence is doing
  pastoral work and is the one most worth getting right. Also *"a gathering
  where nobody knows you and sit at the back"* — check it suits a reader with
  no church to go back to.

## Crisis configuration

In `safety.crisis`. Verified **2026-10-06**, due for review by **2027-04-06**;
`npm run help:verify` fails once that date passes, so the resources cannot go
stale into a release.

- **Territory:** United States only. 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, administered
  by Vibrant Emotional Health with SAMHSA. Call 988, text 988, chat at
  chat.988lifeline.org; Spanish by calling 988 and pressing 2 or texting AYUDA;
  TTY via relay or 711 then 988; ASL by dialling 988 from a videophone.
- **Read from:** 988lifeline.org (three pages) and samhsa.gov. Each source
  records what it confirmed.
- **Outside the US:** no number is printed. The reader is told to contact local
  emergency or crisis services. A directory assembled from memory is the one
  wrong answer this feature must never give.
- **Emergency wording** is the app's own plain sentence and says so.

**To review:** whether US-only is acceptable for a first release given the app
ships Spanish, German and Chinese Scripture, and whether the quiet home entry
("Need urgent help?") is worded right. The configuration offers it from the
Help Me home always, and contextually on two paths only — *Heavy Heart* and
*Guilt & Repeated Sin*.

## The rest of V1

Five paths are outlined and unauthored: Heavy Heart (4 steps), Fear &
Uncertainty (4), Guilt & Repeated Sin (4), Hurt, Anger & Forgiveness (4),
Direction & Decisions (4). Each records what it covers and, where it matters,
the safety rules that bind it — forgiveness never requiring a reader to stay in
danger, and direction never claiming God is telling them which option to pick.

Deliberately **not** created: a separate path per feeling. Depression, anxiety,
loneliness, grief, doubt and shame inform the language inside the broad paths
rather than becoming a menu of diagnoses.

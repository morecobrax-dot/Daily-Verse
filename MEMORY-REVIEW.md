## The bar

A reader may choose any eligible passage for themselves from the Bible. This
catalogue is different: the app puts these in front of somebody unasked, as
**this week's verse**. Each one has to stand on its own, mean the same thing
alone as it does in its paragraph, and be worth the weeks it will take.

Every passage below was read with the verses around it. **Length** is the
number of units the practice can hide, measured across all seven shipped
editions — the smallest is Chinese, the largest usually German or Spanish.

`npm run memory:verify` proves every reference resolves in all seven editions,
that nothing in the catalogue could hold Scripture, and that no passage exceeds
the limit. It cannot judge whether a passage is wisely chosen. That is this
file.

## The three previously excluded, re-adjudicated

| Reference | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Jeremiah 29:11** | KEEP EXCLUDED | A letter to exiles in Babylon about a seventy-year timeline, most of whom would die there. Jeremiah 29:10-11 *would* be responsible — the seventy years make the audience plain — but it needs the exile explained, and a weekly card does not explain. The form people expect, v.11 alone, teaches close to the opposite of what it says. |
| **Proverbs 22:6** | KEEP EXCLUDED | The issue is genre, not boundary, so no range fixes it. A proverb is a general observation; carried as a guarantee it produces real grief in parents of adult children who left. |
| **Philippians 4:13** | **OPEN — see below** | v.13 alone is a promise about performance, which is not what Paul wrote. **4:12-13 is safe**, and the exclusion recorded in V1 was therefore too broad: it named a safety problem where only a boundary problem exists. |

## The one open decision

**Philippians 4:12-13** — "I know how to be humbled, and I also know how to
abound… I can do all things through him who strengthens me." 2 verses, 20–50
units, resolves in all seven editions, and the contentment framing is entirely
inside the boundary.

**Option A — include it**, in place of 1 Corinthians 10:13 (same theme of
holding up under pressure, and 1 Cor 10:13 is the longest single verse in the
catalogue). The app would then teach the most misquoted verse in
English-speaking Christianity *with its own sentence attached*, which is a
service no amount of commentary achieves as well.

**Option B — leave it out.** Paul already holds 8 of the 22 slots and
Philippians already appears once. A memory catalogue's job is passages worth
carrying, not corrections to popular misreadings.

**Recommendation: Option A.** The corrective value is unusually high, the
boundary is genuinely safe, and the swap keeps both the Pauline count and the
catalogue size unchanged. But this is a product judgment with a visible
consequence either way, so it is yours.

If you choose A, the change is one line in `data/memory.json` — swap the
`1 Corinthians 10:13` entry for `Philippians 4:12-13` — plus a re-run of
`npm run scripture:build` and `npm run verify`. The rotation order already
places that slot between Galatians and Hebrews, so no reordering is needed.

## Catalogue shape

- **Old Testament 7, New Testament 15.** Law 1 · Psalms 2 · Wisdom 1 ·
  Prophets 3 · Gospels 4 · Paul 8 · General 3.
- **Paul holds 8 of 22 (36%).** Flagged rather than cut: every one of the eight
  is a first-rank memory text and no replacement would improve the catalogue.
  Worth revisiting if the catalogue ever grows.
- **No narrative history** after Joshua 1:9 was held. The Torah entry carries
  the OT story-shaped material. Acceptable at this size; a gap worth filling if
  the catalogue grows.
- **Christ and the gospel:** John 3:16, Romans 5:8, Ephesians 2:8-9,
  2 Corinthians 5:17, Matthew 11:28-29 and John 13:34-35 — six of 22, and two
  of them are Jesus speaking about himself.
- **Comfort-shaped themes** fell from 8 of 22 to 5. The catalogue is no longer
  mostly about feeling better.
- **Rotation order** puts no two consecutive weeks in the same book, the same
  canonical group, or the same theme cluster — the wrap from week 22 back to
  week 1 included. A contract holds the book rule.

## What a reviewer does not need to check

Resolution in all seven editions, length, duplicate references, duplicate ids,
the absence of Scripture text, and the rotation covering all 22 before
repeating are all held by `npm run memory:verify` and CONTRACT 55. A build that
passes has settled them.
